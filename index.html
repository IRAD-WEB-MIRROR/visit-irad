New to Irad? Start here

Irad sits in the Talas, Chu and Naryn valleys of Central Asia, between Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan and Kyrgyzstan. Most visitors stay in the Irad Special Economic Zone (ISEZ), the river valleys and road belts that hold the three cities. The mountains and steppe beyond the Qara-Chik line are the Autonomous Tribal Regions, governed by clan councils, and you need their invitation to go there. Iradis are Christians of an old Syriac church, they live by the horse and they will feed you until you beg them to stop.

Where to go

Three cities, two mountain ranges and a long belt of orchards and Silk Road ruins. Most trips begin in Talas and run south over the passes to Iyık Janyl.

Talas

The capital: a Kokand citadel, the Old City lanes, the Great Bazaar and the State Hippodrome.

Iyık Janyl

The holy city on the Naryn: the 1727 cathedral, a buried medieval town and the Palace of Art.

The Fergana Rim

Bazar-Korgon's walnut bazaar and Sunday market, potters, walled farmsteads and the road to Arslanbob.

Nurzhan

The oil city on the Chu: Soviet boulevards, Korean canteens and a music scene born in a boiler room.

Mountains and trekking

Besh-Tash National Park, the Kara-Suu Canyon, and the walnut forests and lakes beyond the line.

The Silk Road

A white-stone caravanserai, underground water channels, a silver-mining town and Bronze Age rock art.

Horse country

Ulak tartysh at the Hippodrome, endurance races, stud farms and riding trips on Chongtor horses.

Entry rules, borders and buses

Getting there

Visa-free for US, EU, UK, Canadian, Japanese and Korean passports. No scheduled international flights, so most visitors come overland from Kazakhstan.

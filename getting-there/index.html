Visas and entry rules

The ISEZ sets its own entry rules.

  • US passports: up to 90 days without a visa.
  • EU, UK, Canada, Japan, South Korea and several other countries: up to 30 days without a visa.
  • Everyone else: an entry permit, from an ISEZ trade office abroad or on arrival at Talas.
Ask for the slip. Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan and Kyrgyzstan don't recognise ISEZ entry stamps, and travellers have been questioned at those borders afterwards. Border officers will stamp a separate slip instead of your passport if you ask.

Flying to Irad

There are no scheduled international flights. The charter airline Iradiya flies to its terminal beside Talas Air Base from Almaty, Tashkent, Dubai and Istanbul. Days and times are on the flight schedule of the Talas Civil Air Terminal, with check-in and baggage rules on its passengers page.

Overland from Kazakhstan

Most visitors fly to Almaty or Shymkent in Kazakhstan and cross by land:

  • Chu Gate, on the motorway east of Nurzhan, from the Almaty side.
  • Talas North Terminal, a road and rail crossing at the top of the Trans-Talas Highway, 194 km from Talas.
  • Kum-Shagyl, seasonal and mostly for local traffic.

Customs allowances depend on the crossing, on how long you were away and even on who is in the car. Check your bag on the Customs Service's What can I bring in? page before you set off, and keep the paperwork for honey, kurut and any second phone.

By train: Irad Temir Jolu runs one passenger train a day between Talas and Nurzhan. It takes the long lowland route round the end of the Kyrgyz Range and is about seven hours, against four by road, so it's slow but restful. No railway reaches Iyık Janyl, and there are no international passenger trains, so you cross into Kazakhstan by road. Times and fares are on the Irad Temir Jolu timetable.

Buses to Iyık Janyl cross the Fergana Gateway (Ala-Bel pass, 3,184 m) and passengers' IDs are checked at the summit, so keep your passport on you. Iradiya also flies a short scheduled hop from Talas to Tash-Kömür, 8 km from Iyık Janyl. In summer the Kök-Bel road is the short way from Talas to Nurzhan, but it crosses about 28 km of tribal pasture. Before you set off, check the roads on Kartalar, Attelkom's map.

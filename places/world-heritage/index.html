In 2004 the Church of St. Janyl and the Ruins of Old Iyık Janyl were inscribed on the World Heritage List together, as one property. Irad was still a territory then, so the United States made the nomination on its behalf. The cathedral was 277 years old that year, and the town across the river had been empty for six centuries.

What's on the list

  • The Church of St. Janyl (1727) on Cathedral Hill, with its cloister, seminary, library and archives. It's an East Syriac church built by masons who used Islamic methods: fired brick, a tiled dome and honeycomb vaulting, with Nestorian iconography inside. In the State Register of monuments it is entry No. 97/001.
  • Old Iyık Janyl (Shar-i-Kuhna), the walled 11th-century Nestorian town of Sarvakath on the west bank, straight across the Naryn from Cathedral Hill. Register entry No. 97/002.

The archeparchy owns and runs the cathedral precinct. The state's Heritage Protection Service answers for the listed property, approves any work on the old fabric and pays for part of the conservation. The cathedral marks its status with a plain line at the foot of its website, and its 3D virtual tour was made under the World Heritage management plan.

Visiting the cathedral

The precinct is a working place of worship. Do what the sacristans tell you. The Heritage Protection Service's rules for Cathedral Hill:

  • No photographs or filming inside the church during services.
  • No flash, tripods or lights anywhere inside the church, the library or the archives.
  • No photographs in the seminary library or the cathedral archives at any time.
  • No drones anywhere over Cathedral Hill. The buffer zone runs down to the foot of the hill.
  • Filming and commercial photography need the archeparchy's written consent and a filming permit from the Service.

Service times and the feast calendar are on the cathedral's site. Dress modestly, and come early in the day if you want the church quiet.

Visiting Old Iyık Janyl

The ruins are about 2 km south-west of the centre on the river terrace. You can take photographs for your own use from the paths. Keep to the marked paths and don't climb the walls. Metal detectors are banned in the zone and for 500 m around it. Anything you find on the ground belongs to the state and goes to the warden.

The Silk Road sites, 2014

The Caravanserai of Rabat-i-Safed and the Kok-Tash Kariz went onto the list in 2014 as part of the Silk Roads serial listing, but as Kazakh and Kyrgyz components. Irad is not a State Party to the convention, and the Office of the Governor's protest of 2014 still stands. The Heritage Protection Service runs Rabat-i-Safed as an open-air museum: daily 9:00 to 18:00 (10:00 to 16:00 from November to March), 20 som, with schoolchildren and pensioners free. Most of the kariz lies inside the restricted zone of the Qara-Chik line, where photography is banned. You can see the restored wells from the road. More on our Silk Road page.

Getting there

Iyık Janyl is 347 km south of Talas on State Route 1, about seven hours by bus. Our Iyık Janyl page has the buses, the Pilgrimage Hostel and the guesthouses below the hill.

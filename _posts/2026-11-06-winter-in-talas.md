The first snow has been over the Fergana Gateway, the last football of the year is played tomorrow, the Irad Cup final at the Silk Road Arena on 7 November, and Talas turns the corner into winter. Most travellers come between May and September. They miss the season our members like best.

## What closes

Upcountry, winter is real. The Kök-Bel road to Nurzhan stays open but needs chains or winter tyres, and heavy snow can shut it for a few days at a time; most people take the lowland road or the daily train instead. The old road over the Otmek Pass above the tunnel closes for the season, and the high valleys, the walnut forests and the summer pastures are effectively out of reach. Day trips to the tribal side need more planning and more warm clothing than most visitors carry.

## What stays open

Almost everything in the city itself. Bazar-i Kalan trades through the winter, the covered market steaming with bread and hot samsa, and the bazaar is at its busiest on Saturday and Sunday mornings. The Old City's lanes, the citadel mound and its city museum, the National Historical Museum and the Philharmonic all keep their hours. Marshrutkas run whatever the weather (2 som a ride; city bus 14 for the Old City and Semiz).

Two winter pleasures deserve a place on any list:

- **The moncho.** The Kokand-era domed bathhouse in the Old Citadel Quarter, fed by the citadel springs, is the warmest place in Talas on a cold day. Men's and women's days alternate, so check before you go.
- **The chaikhanas.** Round the bazaar the teahouses glow. Tea comes black with milk in the northern way, in small bowls; in the mornings look for tuyak, the overnight broth that winter Talas breakfasts on.

## Why come now

Quiet, first of all. The guesthouses that turn people away in August have rooms, and winter rates are kinder; your hosts have time to talk, to cook, to drive you to the bazaar and show you which stall sells the good dried apricots. Prices across the city are at their gentlest. And the light is extraordinary: cold, dry days, the valley floor white, the Talas Alatau close and sharp to the south.

Pack for proper cold, plan any trip south round our [roads and passes](/roads/) page, and give yourself at least one slow morning in a chaikhana doing nothing at all.
